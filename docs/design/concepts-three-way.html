Where the space goes today

Measured from the current class lists

Nothing here is a layout problem — it's a chrome problem. Every panel is a floating card with its own border, its own 16px padding and its own gap, and several of them are cards inside cards. Stack five of those down one column and the settings get squeezed into whatever's left.

  • 80px The header — five buttons that each stack a 20px icon over a 12px text label, plus 12px padding top and bottom. The labels repeat what the tooltips already say. Header.tsx
  • ~200px Card chrome in the right column — five panels × (2px border + 24–32px vertical padding), plus the gaps between them. Before a single setting is drawn. App.tsx · right Panel
  • ~50px Cards inside cards — the filter panel wraps a bordered rounded-xl surface in another bordered rounded-xl p-4 surface. Two borders and two paddings for one boundary. FilterStepPanel.tsx
  • ~150px Progress + console headers — a full card carrying four numbers (message, percent, fps, ETA) at text-base, and a second card whose only job when collapsed is a title row. ProgressPanel.tsx
  • ~150px The drop zone — a 32px icon and three lines of instructions kept on screen permanently, long after a file has been loaded and the instructions stopped being useful. VideoInputPanel.tsx
  • 56px The action row — py-4 px-6 on three side-by-side buttons, at the bottom of a column that has to scroll to reach them. ActionButtons.tsx
~430px

of vertical space spent on borders, padding and repeated labels rather than on controls — roughly half the height of a 900px window. All three concepts below spend that budget differently; none of them removes a feature.

Concept A

Console

same layout · ruthless chrome diet

Your layout is fine. Put the tool buttons on a vertical rail, collapse the right column's five floating cards into one continuous surface with sticky section headers, and give every number and every action a single 44px bar at the bottom of the window.

Everything is where it was, just smaller and in fewer boxes.

  • The right column still scrolls once you have 6+ filters
  • Queue still takes height from the main area when open
Concept B

Pipeline

same chrome as A · the filter chain gets an editor model

Filters run in order, so draw them as a chain with pinned endpoints — source at the top, encode at the bottom. Selecting a step opens a docked inspector below instead of expanding in place, so the chain never reflows while you're editing it. Segment selection becomes a scrubber under the preview.

Change to muscle memory
Same controls, new editing model for the one panel you spend all your time in.

Concept C

Workbench

three panes · queue absorbs the input · settings become tabs

The queue and the file picker are the same idea, so make them the same panel — a media list on the left where one file is a list of one. Settings move into a tabbed inspector on the right, which removes the long vertical scroll entirely. The preview gets the whole middle.

The grey, and what sits on it

Everything above recolours live

The base is one HSL ramp rather than six hand-picked hexes, so the whole app can be nudged warm or cool from a single pair of values. Cool keeps a faint blue bias that reads as "screen"; True is dead neutral and the most severe; Warm leans toward the paper end and softens the dark UI. Try them in the bar at the top — the mockups follow.

Six candidates for the secondary

The real constraint isn't taste — it's that Vapourkit already spends colour on meaning. Green is "valid", amber is "warning / privacy / engine building", red is "error / stop", orange is "benchmark / stopping". An accent that lands on top of one of those makes the interface lie. Each card says which one it collides with. Click to apply it everywhere on this page.

Semantic colours stay separate

Whichever secondary you pick, these four keep their jobs and don't shift with it. That's what makes the accent free to be decorative.

Valid · complete · engine built Warning · privacy on · building engine Error · stop · destructive Accent — active, selected, primary action

What this becomes in the config

The current tailwind.config.js hard-codes dark-bg, primary-blue, primary-purple and accent-cyan, which is why the blue-purple is sprayed across 30 files. Replacing them with a numbered neutral ramp plus one accent means the next colour change is a two-line edit. 











  

    
What I'd build

  

  

    

      Concept A, then Blayout
      
        A and B share the same chrome — the rail, the 34px strip, the flat right column, the 44px action bar.
        That's where nearly all of the ~430px comes from and it touches Header, ProgressPanel, ActionButtons
        and the panel wrappers without rewriting any logic. Ship that first, live with it for a week, then
        decide whether the filter panel wants B's chain-plus-inspector model. B is a clean addition on top of
        A, not a different road. C is the strongest layout but it merges two panels and hides state behind
        tabs; that's worth doing deliberately, not as part of a restyle.
      
    

    

      Cool grey--gh 220 · --gs 5%
      
        Neutral enough to read as grey, with just enough blue in it that the greys look chosen rather than
        left at default. True neutral is the more severe option if you want zero hue in the UI at all.
      
    

    

      Teal or Steelsecondary
      
        Teal is the only warm-free hue that doesn't sit on an existing semantic — the nearest neighbour is
        success green and it stays distinguishable at 10px. Steel is the austere answer: barely an accent at
        all, where "active" is just a lighter, slightly blue grey and colour is reserved entirely for
        meaning. Ember is the most striking of the six, but taking it means moving benchmark and stopping
        states off orange first.
      
    

    

      Order of workimplementation

        primary-blue / primary-purple /
        accent-cyan / dark-* class names → Header becomes
        rail + strip → ProgressPanel and ActionButtons merge into the bottom bar → right column loses its
        cards → filter rows tighten. Each step is independently shippable and independently revertable.
