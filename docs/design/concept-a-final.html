Where the video info pane went

VideoPanel.tsx

It was a collapsible card in the right column, below Output Settings — and I left it out of the first mockup. It's back, but not as it was. The panel's whole job is comparing ten values in five pairs, and the current layout puts each pair in two different columns, at different vertical positions, so you read down one side and then down the other and hold five numbers in your head. Putting each pair on one row does the comparison for you, and colouring only the rows that actually differ means the answer to "what is this pipeline changing?" is visible without reading anything.

Same ten values, paired on one line each. Accent marks a real change, grey marks a pass-through — so an interlaced source that stays interlaced, or a resize that didn't take, shows up as a missing colour.

One extra thing it can carry for free: the current panel shows N/A for every output field until a video is loaded and the filters resolve. Since the ledger is derived from the filter chain anyway, an unresolved row can just say pending in dim grey rather than presenting five N/As as if they were data.

Three things these changes decide for you

Not objections — just consequences worth agreeing on before I write any of it.

  • The "editing queue item" banner goes away

    Today editingQueueItemId needs a banner because the queue can be hidden while you're editing one of its items. With the queue as a persistent selectable list, the selected row is the banner — and if the pane is collapsed, the Source section header still says which item you're on.

  • SegmentSelector loses its card but keeps its brain

    The 533-line component has frame-accurate inputs, duration maths and preview seeking. Those all survive — the scrubber becomes the primary control and the popover keeps the numbers. The part that actually disappears is the card wrapper and its header, not the functionality.

  • Three panes plus a rail needs about 1150px

    46 rail + 240 queue + 384 settings leaves 480px of preview at 1150. Below that the queue should auto-collapse rather than squeeze the preview — same trigger you'd use when the window is restored small. Worth deciding whether it re-opens when the window grows back, or stays where you last put it. My vote: stays where you put it, since it's an explicit toggle.

Order I'd build it in

Each step ships and reverts on its own
  1. Tokens. Neutral ramp plus one accent in the Tailwind config, then a codemod over the primary-* / accent-cyan / dark-* class names. Nothing moves; everything changes colour.tailwind.config.js
  2. Rail and strip. Header splits into a 46px icon rail and a 34px data strip. Biggest single win, smallest single diff.Header.tsx · App.tsx
  3. Bottom bar. Progress and the action cluster merge into one 44px bar; the progress line becomes the bar's top edge; console becomes a drawer.ProgressPanel · ActionButtons
  4. Flatten the settings column. Five cards become sections with sticky headers and hairlines; the card-in-card in the filter panel goes.App.tsx · Filter/Output panels
  5. Queue to the left pane. Move QueuePanel out of the vertical PanelGroup into a collapsible horizontal one; retire the editing banner.QueuePanel · App.tsx
  6. Scrubber and segment. New scrubber under the preview; SegmentSelector becomes its popover.SegmentSelector · VideoPreviewPanel
  7. Ledger. VideoInfoPanel becomes five paired rows with change highlighting.VideoPanel.tsx

Steps 1–4 are the ~430px. Steps 5–7 are the two changes you asked for plus the answer to your question, and none of them depends on the others.
